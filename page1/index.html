Stakes

Ready to buy. Still working to pay.

Picture a 47-year-old VIP member opening the app at 11 pm. She’s picked her item and has points ready to spend.

She taps Buy and then spends 42 seconds scrolling through a form to confirm things she already knows.

72% of shoppers who reached that form never finished paying.

Problem

Why we started

The purchase conversion rate was 28%, and customers were dropping off during checkout.

Our goal was to reduce checkout friction and help more customers complete their purchases.

Before

What we found

The existing purchase journey took an average of 42 seconds, from selecting a product to
completing payment. Analysing this journey revealed that customers were dropping off at the payment stage.

Purchase journey

Key checkout issues

  1. A payment button that doesn’t stay visible
  2. A long page to scroll through
  3. Discounts, points and payment are spread across sections

STEP 01

Shipped a bottom-sheet checkout in three weeks instead of rebuilding the form

Why this direction?

Development constraints

The existing checkout was tightly coupled with membership and payment logic. A full redesign would have increased development time, cost and the risk of disrupting existing payment flows making it impractical within the launch timeline.

Benchmarking and design direction

Informed a bottom-sheet approach that would let customers check out without leaving the product page. I prioritised speed and a simpler flow over a complete restructuring of the checkout experience.

Why Choose Option B ?

Option A

Preserved Structure PDP : Fixed check out btn

Retain the existing form structure and keep the
Pay button fixed at the bottom, so customers can
access the payment action without scrolling to the end.

Trade off
Lower implementation effort and risk, but usability
remains largely unchanged.

Option B

Bottom sheet, Reduced information

Added a Buy Now button to the PDP.
Tapping it opens a checkout bottom sheet with only
the essential information, allowing customers to complete their purchase.

Trade off
Prioritised a faster, simpler checkout experience at the cost of additional development time.

STEP 02

Interviewed 10 customers and found we’d ordered the information backwards

Why this direction?

After we introduced the simplified payment UI, customers were still hesitating. So I interviewed 10 of them.

User interview insights

Interviews indicated that customers primarily focused on the final payment amount, applied discounts, and delivery information when confirming payment.

We interviewed 10 users in their 20s–40s, with a range of usage frequencies for the simplified payment service.

We made checkout fast.

A month after launch, GA4 showed that 43 of 250 completed orders were cancelled and placed again.

We’d optimised for speed and removed the two things
our core buyers most wanted to verify:

Their discounts and their points.
Speed was never the whole problem. Trust was.

Trading compactness for transparency

After testing three approaches, we chose the dropdown to keep discount details visible within the checkout flow, trading a more compact layout for greater transparency.

Tooltip

Quick glance, minimal interruption

Discount details appear in a compact tooltip within the checkout screen.

  • Easy to view in one place.
  • Cannot display all available benefits because it does not support scrolling.

Full popup

Complete visibility, clear messaging

A full-screen popup provides a complete view of all discount details.

  • Displays all benefits with a clear information hierarchy.
  • Requires an extra action to close the popup and return to checkout.

Dropdown

Detailed information, seamless flow

Discount details expand inline within the checkout screen.

  • Keeps all key information visible within a single checkout screen.
  • Requires no additional navigation, maintaining a smooth payment flow.

User interview insights

Flexible point value

Converting leftover gift card balances into H.Point increased perceived value by allowing customers to use points both online and in-store.

Seamless H.Point usage

Users valued being able to apply H.Point directly without moving to a separate order screen.

Clear discount visibility

Seeing discount benefits upfront reduced hesitation and helped users complete purchases more confidently.
